      Technical specifications

      Clear expectations before files change hands.

      Accepted inputs Count matrix, normalized matrix, Salmon/quantification summaries, metadata, selected public datasets
      Core analyses Differential expression, PCA/heatmap/volcano, pathway/GSEA review, alternative splicing event summaries
      Visualization Publication-ready static figures with clean labels, legends, dimensions, and export formats
      Statistical notes Contrast definition, filtering assumptions, adjusted p-values, ranked lists, and method limitations
      Deliverables Tables, figures, scripts/notebooks when appropriate, and a concise report or methods summary
      Scope boundary Research support only; no diagnosis, treatment guidance, or patient-specific medical interpretation

      Project handoff

      What makes a dataset ready for analysis.

      Minimum inputs

      • Raw count matrix or accepted quantification outputs
      • Sample metadata with groups, batches, and contrasts
      • Organism, genome/reference version, and gene annotation notes

      Useful context

      • Target genes, pathways, or splicing events of interest
      • Required figure formats for thesis, report, or manuscript use
      • Any previous scripts, tables, or analysis decisions to preserve

      Safer sharing

      • Anonymized or public datasets are preferred for first projects
      • Only share data you are allowed to transfer for analysis
      • Clinical interpretation and patient-specific advice are excluded

      FAQ

      Practical details before starting an analysis request.

      Can you work with public or anonymized datasets?

      Yes. Public, simulated, anonymized, or properly authorized research datasets are the safest starting point for early freelance projects.

      What do I receive at the end?

      Typical delivery includes result tables, figure exports, reusable scripts or notebooks when appropriate, and a concise methods/result summary.

      Can you interpret clinical or patient-specific findings?

      No. The service is limited to research support, data analysis, visualization, and reproducible reporting. Clinical decisions are outside scope.

      What makes a project easier to scope?

      A clear sample sheet, group labels, contrast definition, organism/reference version, expected figures, and any previous analysis decisions.

      Research use first

      Services are positioned for research support, data analysis, visualization, and reproducible reporting. Clinical diagnosis, treatment decisions, or patient-specific medical interpretation are outside the scope.

      Start small, communicate clearly

      Ready to discuss a dataset?

      Share the project goal, organism, data type, sample groups, expected outputs, and whether you need figures, tables, scripts, or a short report. Initial projects can be coordinated through trusted freelance platforms or a structured project brief.
